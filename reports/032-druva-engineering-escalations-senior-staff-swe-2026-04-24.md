## A. CV Match — 2.8/5

The Engineering Escalations role is different from the other Druva roles in this batch: it is focused on product reliability, bug fixing, incident handling, and cross-team collaboration — not pure infrastructure engineering. The role requires fixing production bugs, delivering code changes to improve product resiliency/stability, and working with CloudOps, Product, and customer-facing teams.

**Overlap:**
- Production bug debugging and incident response: Stevens has production SWE experience at Acko (insurance SaaS) and Dell — both involve real customer-facing systems
- Cross-functional collaboration: matches Stevens' work with product, design, and infra teams
- Spring Boot / Java backend: partial stack overlap; Druva's product is partially Java-based
- Communication and presentation skills: requirement matches Stevens' profile
- SaaS product engineering: Stevens builds SaaS products (Acko, Dell platforms)

**Gaps:**
- Go is still the dominant language in Druva's product codebase
- No backup/DR domain knowledge — understanding the product deeply enough to debug escalations requires domain familiarity
- No experience with Druva's specific SaaS architecture (multi-tenant cloud backup)
- Senior Staff level (IC5+) requires ability to independently resolve the most complex escalations and mentor others — requires deep seniority

This is the most transferable of the 4 Druva roles because it emphasizes debugging, reliability, and collaboration over specialized systems programming. CV match ~35-40%.

## B. North Star Alignment — 2.5/5

An escalations/sustenance engineering role is a step away from feature development and AI systems work. While it builds debugging depth and cross-team visibility, it doesn't advance Stevens' goal of working on AI-powered product engineering. The domain (backup SaaS) also has no AI trajectory.

That said, escalations roles at mature SaaS companies can be intellectually interesting — complex distributed system bugs, customer-impacting incidents, architectural trade-off analysis. The work has more human interaction and product context than pure infrastructure. But it still doesn't align with Full-Stack + AI Systems direction.
